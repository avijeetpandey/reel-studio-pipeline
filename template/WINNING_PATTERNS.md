## 1. Hooks (first 2 s)
Seven hook shapes that worked — each is a plain claim, a number or a stake, never a greeting:
1. **Number + promise + scarcity:** "5 Claude skills almost nobody knows about" / "10 hooks that always work".
2. **Everyone vs you:** "Everybody's installing the same Claude skills. Here are 5 that almost nobody knows."
3. **Bold kill-claim:** "Claude just *killed* YouTube." (one italic accent word, red-pen slash on the logo)
4. **Reverse psychology:** "DON'T USE this system — UNLESS you're ready."
5. **New capability (news):** "You can now give Claude Code its own phone number."
6. **Pain → fix:** "If you keep hitting your Claude limit, use this."
7. **Number + low effort + time:** "7 Claude agents you can create in 10 mins" / "…build this weekend".
Visual rule: **the proof is already on screen at frame 0** (the real Connectors page, the ringing phone, the diagram growing) next to the face — the viewer sees the payoff, not a title card. The flop (#9) opened on an empty canvas.

## 2. Structure
- **Hook → Lead → Body → Open loop → Body → CTA** (#11). The lead pays the hook off fast and carries the viewer past ~8–10 s; an **open loop** line ("but that's not the best part", "the catch is…", "the best part:") re-hooks at 50–70 %.
- **Lists win when the progress is visible:** a pinned title for the whole reel (#1, #7), a "SKILL 2 OF 5" kicker + 1-2-3-4-5 stepper (#2), a big numeral beat when the number is said (#6), "DECISIONS MADE 3" / "STEPS 2" counters (#13). Viewers count down → they stay.
- **Best item last**, introduced with "the best part" (#8).
- **Story-demo** beats a dry list (#13): show one real task end-to-end, then "here's how to set it up" in 2 steps, then "the catch".
- Long reels (60–90 s) only work when every 8–12 s delivers a new numbered payoff. the creator's 25–35 s cap stays: use **3 items** (or 5 at ≤ 5 s each) and spend the saved time on proof.

## 3. Proof and visuals
- **Real product, really working:** screen recordings of the actual app (claude.ai, Claude Code terminal, Gmail, GitHub), real outputs (a funnel chart, "All 4 tests passed", a filled lesson plan). Mock UIs only when they look product-grade and show the *output*, never a generic icon.
- **Layouts that recur in the winners:**
  - **Split** (graphic/screen top ~45 %, face bottom ~55 %) with captions on the seam — #2, #3, #6, #14.
  - **PiP** (diagram owns 70 %, face small in a rounded box) — #12.
  - **Full-graphic cutaways** of 2–4 s between face shots — #2, #14.
- **Design systems are consistent within a reel:** one background (cream paper / graphite / lavender gradient), one accent, a serif or heavy-sans title face + a mono label face, the same card radius and shadow everywhere.
- **Typographic accent:** one meaning-word per line in *italic serif* or a second colour ("Claude just *killed* YouTube", "a *manager*", "pick *Opus*").
- **Diagrams that build node by node** (#6, #12) and tiny state changes (✓ ticking, counters rising, a cursor clicking) keep the frame alive — something visibly changes every 1–3 s.
- **Characters:** a pixel mascot (#8) or a cartoon avatar (#13) gives faceless reels a face. For the creator the face IS the differentiator (#9 proves a faceless copy fails without one).

## 4. Captions
- Split/PiP reels: **2–3 words, uppercase mono or bold sans, in a dark box on the seam** (#2, #3, #6, #12).
- Talking-head reels: hook as **one huge word at a time** (≈ 140 px, key word in yellow) for the first 2 s (#15), then small calm captions.
- Kinetic subtitles in faceless reels: current word dark, upcoming words grey (#5, #8, #9) — readable at a glance.

## 5. Sound
- Every winner is "Original audio": voice first, light bed, **SFX on motion** (pops on cards, clicks on cursor actions, whoosh on scene changes). #15's caption says it outright: sound effects keep viewers engaged.
- No trending songs. the creator's rule (YouTube Audio Library bed ≈ −20 dB under the voice) matches.

## 6. CTAs
- **In-video CTA cards** beat a spoken ask: a Follow card with a cursor clicking Follow → "Following" (#3), a "Follow for the builds" pill (#2, #6), a "Save this → Saved" button clicked by a cursor (#11), a comment composer typing the answer (#14), a DM notification sliding in (#3, #13).
- **Comment-keyword-for-DM** (Comment "post" / "agent" / "BUILD") is the single biggest comment driver in the set: comments = 70 %–400 % of likes (#3, #4, #5, #7, #8). BUT it needs (a) a real freebie, (b) DM automation (ManyChat-style), (c) an audience — the creator's week-1 keyword CTAs got 0 comments. Shares are lower on those reels. → **Decided by the creator (24 Oct 2026): don't use keyword-for-DM CTAs.** Every reel and carousel keeps the yes/no question as the CTA; the in-video Follow/Save/comment-YES cards below go with it.
- **Save play** (#11, #1): framework / reference reels end with "Save this" and get saves + shares instead of comments.
- Follow-gating ("follow first so the DM reaches you", "the system only sends it to followers") converts commenters into followers.

## 7. Covers (the uploaded thumbnail, not frame 0)
- Winners use a **designed cover that states the promise**, often different from frame 0: "5 Claude skills barely anyone knows about" with 5 numbered tiles (#2), "these are the 5 MCP Servers you need right now" (#5), "The perfect structure for short-form" (#11), "Engaging Talking Heads pt.2" with the face (#15), "10 Claude systems you can build this weekend" over a lifestyle photo (#4).
- Formula: **number + topic + benefit/scarcity, ≤ 8 words**, 2-level type (small lead-in + big key phrase), the face OR the hero visual, a series tag when it's a series ("pt.2").
- The flop's cover (#9) is a blank grey frame with a play button. **Never upload a cover without a headline.**

## 8. Captions (post text) and tags
- Line 1 = the hook / the keyword (and the CTA when it's a keyword CTA).
- Then the **whole list as text**, numbered (1️⃣… or 1.), one line of benefit each — searchable, saveable, and it answers "where can I find these?" comments.
- One **trust line** ("It writes, you upload. Nothing gets published on its own." / "Real tips, no hype").
- 3–5 tags: the product (#claude #claudeai #claudecode), the niche (#aitools #aiagents), the topic (#youtubetips). Never generic (#ai #fyp #reels).

## 9. Carousels
- **Cover:** number + benefit + time frame ("10 Claude systems you can build this weekend"), big italic numeral, a real photo of the creator at work or a tactile flat-lay (#4, #10) — human, not AI.
- **Slide 2 = proof/credibility** (insights screenshot: "1.6 million views, 51,000 saves") before the how.
- **Roadmap slide** ("there's 5 steps to this: 1)…5)") then one step per slide.
- **Recipe slide template:** "Build N" (accent) · name (bold) · one-line outcome (grey) · a REAL screenshot of it working (one red-pen circle on the key detail) · the tools as logos joined by "+" · footer "@handle" left + "save for later 🔖" right.
- **CTA in the middle too** (#10 slide 7), and again at the end.

## 10. What the flop teaches (#9)
Same look as two winners, 26 likes: empty first frame, soft instruction hook with no stakes, 72 s for 4 basic settings, long near-empty frames, AI-cloned voice, no proof, "I'm not the expert". **Style doesn't transfer results — a sharp claim, real proof, density and a real person do.**

# Round 2 — 22 more reels, studied with audio (24 Oct 2026)
Notes + numbers: `$STUDIO/research/refs_2026-10b/NOTES.md`. These rules extend (and where they conflict, override) the round-1 rules above.

## 11. Voice and script (learned from the transcripts)
- **Talk faster, cut every breath.** Proven reels run 167–246 words per minute (median ≈ 215). Target **95–120 spoken words in 30 s** (≈ 200 WPM): short sentences, no throat-clearing, jump cuts on every pause.
- **Credibility in the first 5 s** after the hook: one line of proof ("I used this in 3 videos that crossed 1M views", "it has 60,000 GitHub stars", "I tested it on my own repo").
- **Talk like a person reacting, not a narrator:** live reactions while showing ("okay this is actually good", "this is going to eat my credits"), mild skepticism, an honest verdict at the end ("I thought this was hype — it's real"). Humour wins (the 70K skit).
- **Script shapes that won:**
  1. **Identity reframe list** (493K): "'You X' → so do Y" × 6–10, then the emotional turn ("that thing they criticise is your superpower"). Career version for the creator: "'You ask too many questions' → become a debugger…".
  2. **Mantra** (153K): one phrase repeated as the spine ("go all in"), 20–25 s, urgency.
  3. **Fact or Hype? series** (37K + 12K): show someone's viral AI claim → test it live on screen → FACT/HYPE verdict. A recurring franchise = a reason to follow.
  4. **Skit / characters** (70K): a tiny story where the tool solves a funny problem; dialogue captions.
  5. **Framework built live** (35K, 19K, 8K): name the framework, reveal parts one by one (blur the unrevealed ones), show it applied to an example.
  6. **Money saved list** (6K): "9 free tools that replace $X of paid software" with a running savings counter.
  7. **Demonstrate the thing** (31K): when the topic is a sound, a font or a UI, the edit itself is the demo.
- **Hook lines that worked (verbatim shapes):** "Don't open X again before you have these 4…", "You can now run X completely free…", "Someone just released a free repo that…", "Before you risk a single dollar, this…", "These are the ONLY 6 words you need to…", "I want to teach you how to…", "Claude edited this entire video", "stop DMing me 😭 it's here".

## 12. Looks that worked (pick per reel, don't mix more than two)
- **A. Lo-fi selfie** (kitchen/table, phone in hand, small pinned title or quote at the top) — the 493K and the 20K list reels. Authentic, fast to film.
- **B. Cinematic studio**: dark room, warm practical lights, bokeh, visible mic, teal/orange grade; small single-word captions; giant kinetic headline words (mixed fonts: heavy condensed + italic script) for 2–3 key moments.
- **C. Graphic top / face box bottom**: dark or cream canvas with real UI (terminal, GitHub cards with ★ counts, struck-through prices), numbered slots, counters; face in a rounded box.
- **D. Reaction / stitch**: the claim clip in PiP + host's skeptical pose + verdict checkboxes.
- **E. Prop as screen**: hold a laptop/phone showing the slides — the graphic lives in the real world.
- **F. Animated skit** (3D/2D characters) — only for story reels.
- Always: frame 0 already has the topic text + a face mid-gesture; something changes every 1–2 s; colour grade consistent per reel.

## 13. Motion and data devices
- Counters (tokens, $ saved, stars, $ earned), progress bars (94 % → "90 % unused"), a bottom progress track of the structure, FACT/HYPE checkboxes, struck-through paid price, **blurred unrevealed list items**, floating viewer comments/DMs as social proof, word-synced bar charts.
- Big type is used for 2–4 moments per reel, not every line; captions stay small the rest of the time.

## 14. Sound (measured)
- Everything sits at ≈ −14 LUFS. About half keep a **constant low music bed** (audio floor ≈ −25 dB under the voice); none let music compete with the voice.
- Energy comes from **speech speed + cuts + SFX**: whoosh on transitions, riser before the payoff, camera-flash on a cut, "vine boom"/hit on the punchline, right/wrong dings on verdicts (viewcci: "pick two or three and use them the same way every time").
- Music must be energetic from the first second (the creator, 24 Oct) — use `scripts/music_energy.py` + the approved library (12 energetic YouTube Audio Library tracks added 24 Oct, each with its best start point).

## 15. Captions, tags, covers
- Post caption: line 1 = the hook or the claim; then the list/steps as text; a trust line ("not sponsored", "nothing runs without your yes", "not financial advice"); the yes/no question; **an SEO keyword line at the end** (plain phrases separated by " · " that people would search: "free alternatives to paid software · open source tools · …"); 3–5 specific hashtags.
- Covers: most proven reels use the **hook frame itself** as the cover (face + readable title in frame 0) — so frame 0 must work as a thumbnail; designed covers ("STOP PAYING FOR CLAUDE CODE", "BUILD PROJECTS ON AUTOPILOT") put 3–5 huge words over the face/hero object.
- Series labels on the cover ("pt.2", "Fact or Hype?") make the grid read as a show.

# Round 3 (19 reels, 25 Oct 2026) — your own study notes live in $STUDIO/research/

## 16. Topic and script
- **The viewer's own problem beats a tool tour, every time.** Top 6 = "how you look on camera", "your talking heads are boring", "an AI stole my job", "how your home is marketed" (17K–56K likes). Bottom 6 = "this skill installs skills", "MCPs you need", "Claude in one weekend" (17–740). Phrase every topic as the viewer's problem first, with the tool as the fix ("Your Claude forgets everything → here's the fix"), never "X tool does Y".
- **Persona twist hook** (51K): say the stereotype, then flip it in the next line. "I'm a software engineer in Bangalore and an AI stole my job. That's what I'd say if I were mid." Your version: "I'm a [your job] and AI already does half my work. Here's the half it can't."
- More hook shapes that won: "If you've been struggling with X, try this." · "This is how we're going to make your X less boring." · "Here's why X gets you ignored." · "Instead of Netflix tonight, watch these 3." Retire "If you're using X and not using this one Y, you're using X wrong" and "master X in one weekend" (both flopped this round; viewers have heard them).
- **Specifics make it believable:** a day, a number, a named object ("on Tuesday it booked the studio", "minute 9 to 12 of that video", "mine runs at 6 a.m.").
- **Series framing** gives a reason to follow: "Day 3 of…", "part 1 of 3", a named franchise ("Fact or Hype?", "AI Maxxing"). A next-episode tease ("next video: how to talk to camera") may sit right before the yes/no CTA.
- **Explain one real concept plainly** (the 51K reel taught "symbol grounding" in two lines). One proper term, explained in everyday words, makes the viewer feel smarter, and they share it.

## 17. Show the change, don't describe it
- **Physical before → after on camera**: switch the light on while saying it; open the bad version, then the good one; Raw | My Edit side by side (17K on an 11 s reel). Every "do X" line should have a frame where X visibly happens.
- **Location / outfit jumps** per sentence (19.7K, 33 cuts/min): when a list reel has no screen content, film each item in a different spot.
- **Over-the-shoulder b-roll of yourself working** (hands, screen, desk from behind): 2–3 shots per shoot. Real, and better than any stock clip.
- **Pop-culture moments**: never film/TV clips (copyright). Use a quote card (serif, the line plus "— Dune") or the creator acting the line.

## 18. Graphics that read as "made by a person"
- **Editorial type**: serif body plus an *italic/script accent word*, mixed sizes in one block ("the *third thing* we're going to do"). It replaces the bold-sans "AI look". Use it for 3–5 key phrases.
- **Hand-drawn annotations on the real frame**: a stroke circle, an arrow and a handwritten label pointing at a real object, drawn on as it's said (`hand-annotate.html`).
- **Pinned list header**: the title stays on top, with a "#N" chip that swaps per item, or the full list with the current item lit (`list-header.html`). Use this or `step-progress`, not both.
- **Rolling counters** for any number that grows: stars, installs, users, $ (`count-roll.html`).
- **Literal hook image** for 1 s: the hook's strongest verb drawn as a picture (tape across a logo for "murdered", a struck-through "CHATBOT").
- **Proof is the real artefact**: the real YouTube thumbnail, the GitHub URL chip, the actual post screenshot. Rebuild product UIs (phone, notification toast, email draft) as clean HTML mockups on a soft colour canvas, with the face in the lower half.
- **Halftone Collage look** (new treatment): b&w halftone cutouts, one flat yellow block, paper drop-shadows, heavy condensed type, elements sliding in on the beat. Use it for opinion/strategy reels.

## 19. Edit and sound (measured)
- Pace 185–236 WPM (median ≈ 212). In 15/19 reels no gap between words reaches 0.12 s. Cut every breath (`qa_reel.py --audio` flags pauses > 0.25 s).
- **The last spoken word is the last frame** in 13/19 reels: no outro hold, no music tail, no silent logo. The loop restarts straight into the hook. Rule: the video ends ≤ 0.5 s after the final word (`qa_reel.py --audio` checks it). The CTA card appears *while* the question is spoken, not after.
- Music where measurable sits close under the voice (−17 to −21 dB) and is cinematic on story reels. Keep the creator's rule (energetic bed, every reel); for story/philosophy reels the bed may be cinematic and rise in the gaps.

## 20. Post captions
- Long, personal captions did best: the story behind the reel, the gear/setup list, "one thing I forgot to say: …". Add a **"forgot to say"** line or a **setup/gear line** to the caption when it's true. Keep the yes/no question and the SEO line.

# Round 4 (15 reels, 25 Oct 2026) — your own study notes live in $STUDIO/research/

## 21. Give the viewer a role
- **Participation format** (326K, the biggest reel in four rounds): an overlay that makes the viewer a player ("Hold ⬜ to sprint, tap △ to stop me") and one question in the caption ("Which button are you pressing first?"). 10–15 s, loops, no voice needed. Your versions: "Pause when the AI lies", "Tap ⬜ to make ChatGPT confident, △ to make it say 'I don't know'", "Screenshot the moment the prompt breaks". Make one every 2–3 weeks as a break from talking reels.

## 22. Plain everyday-tech explainers, as a series
- **"How the heck does X work?"** (297K) and **"Let's visualise git"** (24K) beat every AI-tool reel. They cover something the viewer uses, what it really does, and **what it does NOT do** (the honest caveat: "VPNs don't make you anonymous"). They use almost no graphics and end on a yes/no question ("Do you use a VPN?").
- Name the series and say it in line 2 ("Welcome to another episode of …"). Use one everyday analogy per item (local repo = "a filing cabinet on your computer").
- **Question hooks are allowed** when they name something the viewer uses and line 2 answers at once ("So how do VPNs work?", "What if you could see your home before it's built?"). Questions about niche tools still fail.

## 23. Edit craft (measured / taken from the AI-edit reels)
- **Lock graphic entrances and b-roll cuts to the music's beat**; speech-tied timing wins. Use `scripts/beat_grid.py bgm.mp3 --check <composition>`, which snaps anything within 150 ms. Put big moments on bar lines (every 4 beats).
- **Grade every frame**: sample one frame per second and score each 1–10 against the taste rules. Redo anything under 8 and any dead frame (qa.md).
- **AI-slop tells (never)**: all-caps centred text on a gradient; glow on everything; neon overload; 3D/cartoon versions of real people; "FREE FOREVER"-style stickers; more than one decoration per beat. The four most decorated reels got the fewest likes.
- **Keep one honest imperfection** (one "um", a laugh). It reads as human.
- **Karaoke caption reveal** (the whole line faint, each word lights as spoken) for calm Notebook / Editorial reels; **outlined giant numerals** (thin stroke) for lists; a **live checklist card** that ticks as each item is said (`check-card.html`).
- Ending: 13/13 talking reels end 0.1–0.6 s after the last word, which confirms §19.

## 24. "This video was edited by AI" genre
- The hook proves itself ("I didn't edit a single second of this video", "Would you believe Claude edited this entire video?"). Devices: a running timecode, "7:08 → 0:39" with the rambling struck through, the checklist of what the AI added, a live change on a spoken command ("make it moody" → grade shifts). You can make this one honestly with this pipeline. Avoid the keyword-for-the-system CTA; ask "Would you let AI edit your videos?".

## 25. Filming and sound
- A wireless mic held in hand as a prop (10/13 talking reels): fine, even good, as long as it is steady and below the chin.
- Movement without edits: walk-and-talk toward the camera, 1–2 establishing shots (a wide of the desk, the street, a drone or high angle) at the start of a section.
- **Digital SFX palette** for UI beats: soft pluck, low pulse, digital sweep, digital tick, soft hit, check blip (all in `assets/audio/sfx/`, generated by `make_sfx.py`). Prefer these over airy whooshes on screen/card moves.
