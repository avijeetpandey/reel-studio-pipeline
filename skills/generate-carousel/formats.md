# Carousel formats — slide recipes

Each recipe lists the slide order with the layout (`type`) to use. Counts include cover and CTA. Keep to 6–10 slides; merge items rather than pad.
Research basis (Oct 2026): hook slide decides most of the outcome; 7–10 slides for how-to; one idea per slide; a summary/checklist near the end drives saves; one clear final ask; recurring series drive follows. Top creators studied: Ruben Hassid (handwritten notebook pages + real tool screenshots), Sahil Bloom (tweet-style text cards, ink illustrations, handwriting), ByteByteGo (clean step diagrams). What they share: they look made by a person.

## Glossary (8–9 slides)
1 `cover` (number + promise, photo) → 2 `statement` (why these words matter; "explained like I would to my mom") → 3–7 `glossary` with 3 terms each, **grouped by theme with a kicker** ("Part 1 · How it reads you") so it reads as a story, not a dictionary — one of them carries the fix as an `eg` or a pen `note` (e.g. sycophancy → "tell it: say when I'm wrong") → 8 `recap` "If you remember only 3" → 9 `cta`.
Definitions ≤ 12 words; add `eg` to at most one term per slide.

## How-to (8–9)
1 `cover` (outcome + time: "Set up your family's anti-scam plan in 5 minutes") → 2 `statement` (why now — one real case) → 3–7 `point` with `n` (one step per slide; first words are a verb) → 8 `checklist` (all steps as ticks — the screenshot slide) → 9 `cta` (share to family/friends).

## Step-by-step / how it works (8–10)
1 `cover` (the moment everyone knows: "What happens in the 2 seconds after you tap Pay") → 2 `steps` overview (3–5 boxes, the whole journey) → 3–7 `point` zooming into one step each (analogy in `eg`, optional real screenshot) → 8 `point` **the fix** (what to do with this knowledge) or `statement` surprise ("The wildest part: …") → 9 `recap` → 10 `cta`.

## Story (7–9)
1 `cover` (the most surprising moment, past tense, with a place/year: "Everyone on the call was fake.") → 2–5 `story` with `stamp` (date · place), one beat per slide, end each beat on tension → 6 `statement` the turn ("Then he called head office.") → 7 `checklist` lessons = **the fix** (what you'd do if it happened to you) → 8 `cta`.
Stories are the best follow-driver: tell it like it happened to a person, not as a news summary.

## Framework (7–9)
1 `cover` (the problem the framework solves) → 2 `statement` why the usual way fails → 3–6 `point` one part each with `n` → 7 `template` or worked example → 8 `recap` → 9 `cta`.

## Checklist (7–8)
1 `cover` → 2 `statement` the stakes (one fact, sourced) → 3–6 `checklist` slides (3 items each, `small` = where/how) → 7 `cta` (share to the person who needs it).

## Comparison (7–8)
1 `cover` (the confusion: "Chatbot, copilot, agent — what's the difference?") → 2 `statement` one everyday scenario used throughout → 3–5 `compare` one dimension each → 6 `statement` the rule of thumb → 7 `cta` ("Which would you trust with your money? Yes or no: …").

## Myth vs fact (8–10)
1 `cover` ("8 AI myths even smart people believe") → 2–8 `myth` with `n`, the fact in ≤ 6 words + one line of proof → 9 `statement` "How many did you believe?" → 10 `cta`. Comment magnet: the yes/no can be about the most surprising myth.

## Explainer / claim scoreboard (8–9)
1 `cover` (the claim in quotes + "Here's the scoreboard") → 2 `statement` what the word actually means → 3 `stat` the headline number → 4 `compare` who agrees / who doesn't → 5–6 `point` context → 7 `recap` how to read any such claim → 8 `cta`.

## Data story (7–8)
1 `cover` (the question the number answers) → 2–4 `stat` one number per slide with `source` → 5 `compare` per-use vs total → 6 `statement` what actually moves the needle → 7 `cta`.

## Timeline (8–9)
1 `cover` ("This started as a gaming chip") → 2–6 `timeline` (3 events per slide, `hot` on the turning point) or one `story` per era → 7 `statement` the why → 8 `cta`.

## Templates (7–8)
1 `cover` (before → after promise) → 2 `compare` bad vs good example → 3 `point` the formula → 4–6 `template` one per situation → 7 `cta` ("save it for your next application").

## Comic (7–8)
Use `story` slides with one line of dialogue each and `stamp` as the panel label ("Panel 1"); the pen `note` is the punchline. Then `point` explaining the real attack, `checklist` defences, `cta`.

## Workflow (personal, 8)
1 `cover` ("My real AI workflow as a senior engineer", photo) → 2–7 `point` one step each, written as "I …" → 8 `cta`. Keep it employer-neutral.

## Recipe list (learned from the reference carousels, Oct 2026 — e.g. "7 ChatGPT settings you can fix in 5 minutes")
- **Cover:** number + benefit + time frame; big numeral; real photo of the creator at work or a tactile flat-lay / the real app — never a blank or stock image.
- **Slide 2:** proof or the promise paid off at once (a real result, a real screenshot).
- **Slide 3 (optional):** roadmap — `steps` layout listing all items 1)…N).
- **Item slides:** `point {n, kicker:"Fix N", title:<name>, body:<one-line outcome>, image:<real screenshot>, note:<red-pen circle label>}`; tools named as "Claude + Gmail" in the kicker or body; footer note "save for later".
- **~60 % slide:** a slim yes/no question strip (`statement` with the question) — the mid-carousel CTA.
- **Last slides:** `recap` (all items, save this) → `cta`.
- Caption: the whole list as numbered text, one trust line, the yes/no question, 3–5 tags.
