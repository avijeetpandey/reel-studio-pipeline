# Carousel copy — hooky, plain, flowing

## Cover hooks (pick one pattern; 5–12 words; a claim, not a topic label)
- **Everyday surprise:** "ChatGPT is trained to agree with you."
- **Number + outcome for a real person:** "15 AI words everyone uses, explained like you're not an engineer."
- **Moment everyone knows:** "What happens in the 2 seconds after you tap Pay."
- **Story opener (past tense, specific):** "Everyone on the video call was fake. Including the CFO."
- **Stakes + time:** "Set up your family's anti-scam plan in 5 minutes."
- **Contrarian, provable:** "AI detectors can't tell if you wrote it."
Avoid: "X tips for Y", "Everything you need to know about…", "The ultimate guide", questions that can be answered "no" and swiped away ("Do you use ChatGPT?").
`sub` (optional) adds who it's for or the payoff: "No maths. No jargon. Just how it actually works."
Cover `note` (red pen): a human aside — "save this one", "I wish I knew this in college", "my mom asked me this".

## Slide 2 — pay the cover off immediately
Give the answer, the stakes or the map. Never "In this post I'll…". Example: "You don't need to code to understand AI. You need 15 words."

## Flow — make the next slide feel necessary
- End some slides on an open line: "But that's not the scary part." / "Here's where it breaks." Use on ~1 in 3 slides, not every slide.
- Number things the viewer is counting (`n`), and show progress in kickers ("Part 2 · How it answers").
- Put one surprise around the middle (a stat, a myth, a "wait, what?").
- Use the same example all the way through when you can (one family, one Goa trip, one bug). Repetition = easy to follow.
- The recap slide repeats the 3 things worth remembering, in the same words as the slides.

## Plain English rules
- Short words, short sentences (≤ 15 words). Active voice. Contractions.
- Explain every technical word the first time with an everyday comparison.
- Daily-life examples from your audience's world (config `audience`) that still make sense elsewhere — e.g. for India: UPI, chai, cricket, the family WhatsApp group.
- Numbers as digits; one number per slide; say where it's from.
- First person where it's true: "I use this at work", "I tested this".

## The fix slide (every carousel)
One concrete action the viewer can take today, written as an instruction: "Settings → Personalization → add: 'Tell me when I'm wrong.'" Use `point` (with `n`), `template` (when it's text to copy) or `checklist`. Red-pen note: "save this". It's the slide people screenshot — keep it to one action, real names of real buttons, no vague advice ("be careful with AI").

## Final slide (`cta`)
- `question`: one debatable yes/no question tied to the topic ("Did you know ChatGPT doesn't look things up?", "Would you let AI pay your grocery bill?"). Never "comment KEYWORD".
- `yn` default "Yes or no? Tell me below." (change only for variety: "Yes or no? I read every reply.")
- `share`: name a specific person — "Send this to the friend who…", "Send this to your parents."
- `follow`: the series promise ("I decode one AI thing every day.") — keep it, it's what makes the follow make sense.

## Banned (they read as AI or as bait)
unlock, master, supercharge, game-changer, revolutionize, leverage, delve, dive in, deep dive, "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note", "here's the thing", "secret", "hack", "you won't believe", "ultimate", "mind-blowing", emoji as bullets, more than one emoji per slide, em-dash chains, Title Case Headlines, exclamation marks on more than one slide, "comment GUIDE/AI/KEYWORD".

## Worked example (C02, slide 3)
kicker: "Part 1 · How it reads you"
- Token — "A piece of a word. AI reads tokens, not letters." eg: "“unbelievable” = un · believ · able"
- LLM — "Large language model. A program that predicts the next word."
- Context window — "How much of your chat the AI can see at once."
Why it works: grouped as a story beat, each definition ≤ 12 words, one example, no jargon left unexplained.
